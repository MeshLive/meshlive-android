# Calls

Part of the [protocol reference](README.md): private-call signalling and in-call link renewal.

## Private-call signaling

`BMC2` is an exact 23-byte payload inside endpoint-content type `6`:

| Field | Bytes |
| --- | ---: |
| Magic `BMC2` | 4 |
| Signal | 1 |
| Random non-zero call ID | 16 |
| Reason | 1 |
| Relay-hop count | 1 |

Signals are Invite (`1`), Ringing (`2`), Accept (`3`), Decline (`4`), Cancel (`5`), Hang-up (`6`), Camera off/on (`7`/`8`) and Microphone muted/unmuted (`9`/`10`). Reasons are None (`0`), Rejected (`1`), Busy (`2`), Unsupported (`3`), Normal (`4`) and Error (`5`). Decline accepts only Rejected/Busy/Unsupported; Hang-up accepts only Normal/Error; all other signals require None. Unknown values, invalid length, zero call ID and invalid state transitions are rejected.

**Ring timeout (2026-10-20, no wire change).** A direct call that is not answered ends: the caller sends Cancel 60 s after the callee's Ringing and shows "No answer"; a callee still ringing after 65 s sends Decline/Rejected (design decision 2026-10-21, the same on every platform; it was 75 s) and shows "Missed call" (longer than the caller's limit, so a current caller's Cancel normally arrives first; the callee limit ends calls from older callers that never cancel). A callee whose caller cancels after 55 s or more of ringing shows "Missed call" instead of "Caller cancelled". Relayed calls keep their 60 s callee cap. Before this, direct calls rang until someone acted (an unanswered call rang for 6 minutes on 2026-10-20, and neither phone could be called by anyone else meanwhile). Control arm `test_call_ring_timeout=false`.

**Hang-up before a liveness close (2026-10-20, no wire change).** A call phone that ends a call because nothing arrived from its peer for 8 s (or, with the Experimental stall tolerance, for up to 14 s while its own writes still complete) first writes Hang-up/Error on the link, best effort and for at most 300 ms, then closes as link lost. A peer that can still read (its own sending had stalled) ends at once instead of after its own 8 s; it tells the user that its sound and video stopped reaching the other phone when its own sends have not completed for 3 s, otherwise it shows the Error hang-up as before. Ringing phases send nothing, as before. Control arm `test_call_liveness_hang_up=false`.

**In-call link renewal (on by default since 2026-10-21).** A direct GATT call whose one direction stalled can replace its hop link and keep the call: see "Experimental in-call link renewal (Design SR, on by default)".

**Busy while dialling (2026-10-19, no wire change).** A callee whose user is dialling someone else declines an Invite at once with Decline/Busy, before any Ringing. "Dialling" means the user's own call is still looking for its person: the address refresh of a tapped row, or a Contacts or Recent call waiting for a sighting. A caller shows Busy as "they are calling someone else or are in another call". Older builds show it as an ordinary decline. An Invite from the very person being dialled (matched by the caller's real node prefix after the handshake; a throwaway never matches) rings as usual and ends the callee's own dial, so two phones that dial each other connect through whichever Invite arrives first.

**Incoming phase timers (Design PP package A, Experimental, off by default; no wire change).** Two local timers observe an incoming connection's milestones and change no record, flag, signal or version. The milestones are: transport accept, first handshake frame (BMH2 only), hop verified (the hop handshake and the required `BDO1` opening), end-to-end handshake complete, reader started and first valid `INVITE`. The hop timer owns transport accept to hop verified on a BLE GATT or CoC connection and never ends at the end-to-end session, so a helper waiting for its next hop or a target waiting for READY and the caller's handshake keeps the relay budgets (SETUP and pre-SETUP 20 s, the endpoint-handshake watchdog) unchanged. The pre-INVITE timer starts when the reader of an authenticated ordinary incoming call starts and ends at the first valid `INVITE`: the timer only observes a signal that already passed framing, sequence, call-ID and identity checks and never relaxes one, and block and dial checks follow as before. A verification-only session (`BVMID2`, which sends no `INVITE`), a message exchange, a relay duty, an outgoing call, a later carrier and a link renewal have no such timer. Each connection has its own generation, so a timer never closes or disarms a later connection; on expiry only that connection is closed, silently, and the endpoint's normal end handling restores availability. Where a transport's timing is unmeasured (Classic, Wi-Fi, a relayed endpoint, a BMH2 hop) the conservative 20 s bound applies. Switches `test_call_preauth_deadline_short` and `test_call_pre_invite_deadline`; see the switch register.

Only Invite can carry a non-zero relay count: the number of relays between caller and callee, which the caller reads from the route cost of the offer it connects to. The wire parser accepts counts `0..15`; the app builds at most four relays (below). Both endpoints validate call ID and state before acting. Camera/microphone start after recipient acceptance. `BMC2` is never accepted as plaintext BLE control.

For the opaque call relay, A and C alone hold content keys. B copies both ordered encrypted directions through independent adjacent queues/liveness without camera, microphone, decode or re-encode. The envelope binds call ID, endpoint identities, direction and endpoint records; adjacent radio identity remains separate from endpoint identity.

### Private-call relay chains

A call may cross a chain of opaque relays: caller -> R1 -> ... -> Rn -> callee, with n at most four (`PrivateCallRelayChain.MAX_RELAYS`; the six required phones form at most four relays between two endpoints). On by default since 2026-10-09; DEBUG `test_call_relay_chain=false` (benchmark tunable `call_relay_chain`) restores the one-relay rule exactly. The call relay itself is DEBUG-only. No record type, flag or signal changes: chains reuse the discovery route cost, the BMH2 link hello's signed `maxChildren`, and the existing Invite relay count. With chains on, a call relay also sets transport-mask bit 6 (`0x40`, call relay) in the capabilities of its signed BMH2 link hello. Every reader tests only the transport bits it needs, so older peers ignore it; with chains off the hello is unchanged.

- **Downstream first, one parent, one child.** A relay authenticates its child with a fresh BMH2 link handshake before it advertises anything. Only then does it advertise the child's offer (the callee's node prefix and flags) at route cost + 1, accept exactly one parent within 180 s (`PARENT_WAIT_MS`: enough for three outer relays and the caller to connect), and leave the air (Classic too, with chains on; CoC and GATT already stopped advertising). A relay whose parent never comes stops and releases its child. Each relay serves one call and stops when either side closes.
- **Child admission (hop-local).** An offer at route cost 0 is the callee itself: the child's authenticated link identity must equal the announced prefix, exactly as for one relay. An offer at route cost 1..3 is another relay: the child's link identity must not be the announced callee (Classic refuses a direct link, as for a relayed caller), must not be this relay, and its signed link hello must declare relay capacity (`maxChildren` at least 1; call endpoints sign 0) and the call-relay bit (forwarding receivers and stream relays also sign `maxChildren` 1 but never the bit). Over Classic a relayed child is only the advertising device itself (its discovered address, or its exact bonded address or unique bonded name): no bonded-phone fallback and no inquiry, so a relay cannot attach to another call's relay or a receiver that happens to answer. A relay never takes an offer for its own prefix or above route cost 3, so no advertised count exceeds four.
- **Parent admission (hop-local).** A relay refuses a parent whose link identity is itself, its own child or the callee it relays for.
- **No loops.** A relay has a child before it advertises, and it takes one parent only after that. A cycle would need some relay to be another's child before that relay had a child of its own, so every chain is a path that ends at a route-cost-0 endpoint. The route cost grows by one at every hop and stops at four. No signal travels more than one hop: each relay reads its child's advertisement and its two adjacent link hellos only.
- **Caller limit.** The caller ignores and refuses an offer whose route cost exceeds four instead of clamping it into the Invite.
- **End-to-end identity.** The caller runs the BMH2 endpoint handshake through every relay and requires the endpoint identity to match the discovered callee prefix ("Call peer identity changed between discovery and connection"); the callee authenticates the caller the same way. Relays copy endpoint handshakes, ciphertext and ordered records unchanged and replace heartbeats with version-1 liveness, as one relay does.

**Malicious relay.** Route cost, prefix and flags in a BLE advertisement are unauthenticated hints, and a relay's signed `maxChildren` proves only that it chose to sign relay capacity. A malicious relay, or a phone that advertises a callee's prefix at any route cost, can therefore drop, delay or refuse the call, attract it to a longer route, or report a false relay count, which changes only the caller's relayed video cap. It cannot impersonate an endpoint: endpoint signatures and keys use their own domain, the node ID is a hash of the identity key, and an endpoint hello that a relay answers itself carries the relay's identity, which the caller's prefix check refuses. It cannot read, forge, reorder or replay media or call signals, because `BME1` and `BMC2` are sealed with endpoint keys it never holds, and a modified record fails the endpoint's open. It sees what one relay sees: adjacent identities, signed public endpoint hellos, and record sizes and timing. The 48-bit discovery prefix is the existing binding between a discovered person and an endpoint identity; chains add no weaker step.

Admission, overload and capacity guards stay hop-local: each relay still holds exactly one parent and one child, the endpoints keep their admission and rate rules, and relayed calls keep the relayed video cap (15 FPS, 110 kbps; an experimental measured cap is in the register in [architecture.md](../architecture.md)). A relay that cannot keep up exerts backpressure on its parent link; a stalled hop ends the call through the existing liveness and close paths.

### Voice calls and push-to-talk

Status (2026-10-11): no longer experimental. Voice calls are on by default since 2026-10-17 with no Settings switch (DEBUG `test_voice_calls` is the benchmark switch, default off there), and hold-Mute-to-talk is part of every call. The wire is unchanged.

Voice calls add no signal, reason, capability bit or version; `BMC2` stays 23 bytes. The only change is ordering: Camera off (`7`) may now precede acceptance. A voice caller queues Invite and then Camera off in its ordered endpoint sequence. A callee with the experiment on treats Camera off while it is ringing as a voice offer; Camera on while ringing withdraws the offer. A voice answer queues Accept and then Camera off. Neither side starts a camera or video encoder, and no video packet, codec configuration or keyframe is sent. Each `BTV3` header still declares Opus audio and the codec/format that a later upgrade would use. A receiver creates its video decoder only when the first video packet arrives.

Existing Android peers, and peers with the experiment off, already accept Camera off/on in every call phase. They see a video call whose caller has video off, and they answer with their own video. The voice caller shows that video when the first frame renders. During the call, each endpoint controls only its own camera with Camera on/off, as in video calls: adding video is an upgrade, and turning off your own video is a downgrade. The voice layout appears while neither direction carries video. A negotiated two-sided switch, where one side asks the other to turn its camera on or off, would need a new signal value. The `BMC2` decoder rejects unknown signal values, so that value would need a capability gate and review; it is left for later.

### Call codec preference bit

In an end-to-end call hello, decode-mask bit 7 (`0x80`) means "decode VP9 when you can encode it; the other listed codecs are decodable fallbacks". A legacy Qualcomm phone (API 32 or older) sets it, with its real decoders, instead of advertising VP9 only. A sender that has a VP9 encoder sends VP9; one that has none sends another listed codec. With HEVC on (Design HC), a sender's hardware HEVC encoder still goes before VP9 when the peer lists HEVC decode. The bit is not a codec: codec tests ignore it, link hellos never carry it, and relays copy the end-to-end hello unchanged, so direct and relayed calls decide alike. The control arm `test_call_codec_fallback=false` advertises VP9 only, as before.

### Experimental call video fragments (off by default)

`test_call_video_fragments` (benchmark tunable `call_video_fragments`) adds one endpoint content type, video fragment (`9`), for direct calls only. Its reason: a GATT write blocks for the air time of the whole record, so call audio waited 620-1193 ms behind one large video frame (calls-noloss-m31asus-51cc8028).

- **Negotiation.** An endpoint with the experiment on sets bit 7 (`0x80`) of the transport mask in its signed end-to-end hello. The link hello is unchanged. Existing decoders accept unknown transport bits and test only the transport bit they need. A sender fragments only when both hellos carry the bit and the call is direct (relay count 0). With the experiment off, neither the bit nor type `9` is ever sent, so the wire is exactly as before. A receiver that did not advertise the bit rejects type `9`; every other endpoint reader (streams, relays, forwarders) rejects it as an unknown type, exactly as before.
- **Format.** A video media packet whose encoded `BTV3` packet (13-byte header plus payload) exceeds 3,072 bytes is cut into chunks of at most 2,048 bytes (the sender chooses the size; see Chunk size). Each chunk is sealed as its own `BME1` record of type `9`, with the frame's packet flags in the header. The payload is a big-endian total length (4 bytes), a big-endian offset (4 bytes), and then the chunk. Fragments use the same directional key, nonce sequence and replay/reorder rejection as every endpoint record, and `BMS1` hop ordering is unchanged.
- **Chunk size (sender choice, no wire change).** The format names each fragment's total and offset and the receiver accepts any positive chunk length up to the frame total, so chunk sizes are not part of the contract. The default sender uses 2,048 bytes. The experimental arm `test_call_fragment_airtime_ms=N` (benchmark tunable `call_fragment_airtime_ms`, default 0 = off, needs `test_call_video_fragments`) instead sizes each chunk as measured link rate x N ms, clamped to 675..4,096 bytes, re-evaluated for every fragment. Every fragment costs 75 bytes of framing and authentication besides its chunk: hop record header 17 and tag 16, endpoint record header 18 and tag 16, fragment header 8 (the 13-byte `BTV3` packet header is paid once per frame). The 675-byte minimum is the smallest chunk with at most 10% of the fragment on the wire being that overhead (2,048 bytes is 3.5%, 4,096 bytes 1.8%). A frame is cut into the fewest, near-equal chunks of at most the target (no runt tail, none below the minimum), and a packet of at most 1.5 x the target chunk goes whole (3,072 bytes for 2,048). The rate is this call link's peak delivery goodput over the last 10 s from the peer's version 3 link reports (which count media bytes, so small fragments do not starve them), else the peer's arrival-train estimate, else this phone's learned single-link probe, which may only shrink the chunk below 2,048; with none of them the chunk stays 2,048. The arrival-train estimate itself needs records of 8,000 bytes or more and therefore stops updating once frames are fragmented. Chunk sizes (rate dependent) are traffic metadata, like fixed sizes.
- **Interleaving.** Between two fragments the sender may write queued call audio media packets, and only those. In the airtime arm the sender also writes all queued audio before the first fragment, so no fragment starts while call audio is queued. The receiver accepts a frame's fragments only in order: the first at offset 0, each next one at the previous end, all with the same total and flags. The flags may not include audio or codec configuration, and the total is at most the largest endpoint plaintext. Once complete, the receiver decodes the concatenation as a `BTV3` media packet with the same flag check as a whole media packet.
- **Failure.** While a frame is unfinished, a non-audio media packet or a stream header fails the call closed, as does any gap, overlap, change of total or flags, empty chunk or overrun.
- **Metadata.** Fragment sizes and timing replace whole-frame record sizes as traffic metadata.

Push-to-talk adds no wire value. Holding the button sends Microphone unmuted (`10`), and releasing it sends Microphone muted (`9`). The microphone and Opus encoder keep running, and the sender drops captured audio while muted. Security is unchanged: all signals use the authenticated, ordered endpoint sequence. As with camera off today, a relay or radio observer can infer a voice call or a released talk button from the absence of video or audio records. That is record size/timing metadata.

## Experimental in-call link renewal (Design SR, on by default)

Switch: `test_call_link_renewal` (on by default since 2026-10-21, design decision; DEBUG `false` is the control arm, on both phones; Android `CallLinkRenewal.kt`). Reasons, options and both reviews: DECISIONS "Design SR". A renewal replaces the **hop link** of a running direct GATT call after a one-way stall; the call ID, the call state and the end-to-end `BME1` session (keys and sequences) continue. It is not a redial. The constants below are part of the contract.

**Scope (checked, not assumed).** Only a call whose end-to-end peer is its hop peer: no relay (relay count 0, no relayed route, the link's authenticated node ID equals the end-to-end node ID), over GATT on both ends (a CoC, Classic or dual-listening call that runs on another transport is not renewed), in the `ACTIVE` phase, never a `BVMID2` verification session. A relayed or other call ends as before. Every renewal record is end-to-end content, so a relay never sees or acts on one.

**Capability.** End-to-end hello transport-mask bit 4 (`0x10`) = "sends and accepts content type 12 and the renewal opening". End-to-end hellos use bits 3 (`BDX1`), 5 (`LABELS`) and 7 (call video fragments); bit 4 is used only in link hellos (relay `SETUP`), a different signature domain, and no end-to-end hello sets it today. An opener accepts type 12 only after its own endpoint signed bit 4 (otherwise it rejects the type as unknown, as every older reader does); a phone sends type 12 only when both end-to-end hellos carry it.

**Records: `BME1` content type 12 `RENEW`.** Flags 0. Payload = op (1) | body, exact lengths:

| Op | Name | Body | Payload bytes |
| ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 1 | `OFFER` | ticket (8) \| role (1): 0 = end-to-end initiator (caller), 1 = responder | 10 |
| 2 | `ARMED` | ticket (8) | 9 |
| 3 | `REFUSED` | ticket (8) \| reason (1) | 10 |
| 4 | `BIND` | ticket (8) \| next send sequence (u64) | 17 |

Reasons: 1 not direct, 2 limit (phase, count or interval), 3 server registration, 4 advertising, 5 collision, 6 disabled, 7 abandoned. `REFUSED` from the armed phone means it will not arm; `REFUSED` abandoned comes from the offerer and means it kept the old link, so the armed phone disarms (stops advertising, resumes its liveness rules). An unknown op, another length, an all-zero ticket, a role other than 0 or 1, or a reason outside 1..7 is a malformed record and fails closed like every `BME1` record. `BIND`'s next send sequence is the `BME1` sequence of the `BIND` record plus one; any other value is malformed.

**Renewal key.** `K_renew = HKDF-SHA-256(IKM = min(k1, k2) || max(k1, k2), salt = "BluetoothVideoMesh/SR1", info = "renew-key", L = 32)`, where `k1`, `k2` are the session's two directional `BME1` content keys and `min`/`max` compare the 32-byte keys as unsigned bytes. Both endpoints compute the same value without a role. It is derived when the end-to-end session is created and only when both end-to-end hellos signed bit 4 (BMH2 and BMH3 end-to-end handshakes alike; no other session holds it), kept only in memory with the session and zeroed when the session closes. The existing content keys and the key schedule are unchanged.

**Advert.** `UUID = HMAC-SHA-256(K_renew, "SR1 srv" || callId (16) || ticket (8))[0:16]` with the version nibble set to 4 and the variant bits to `10`. The armed phone advertises one fresh legacy connectable set carrying only this 128-bit service UUID: no announcement, no manufacturer data and an empty scan response (the set is scannable, like every connectable legacy set of the app), non-discoverable where the platform allows it (API 34+); on API 29-33 the AD flags stay discoverable, which the platform cannot clear (logged `nonDiscoverable=false`). A fresh set has a fresh resolvable private address. The set stops when the renewal is bound, 7 s after `OFFER` arrived, or when the call ends.

**Renewal opening (new GATT link, before any `BMS1` byte).** Fixed-length frames on the GATT byte stream:

```text
armed -> offerer   CHALLENGE  "SR1C" | challenge (16)                              20 bytes
offerer -> armed   PROOF      "SR1P" | ticket (8) | nonce (16) | mac (32)          60 bytes
armed -> offerer   CONFIRM    "SR1A" | mac (32)                                     36 bytes

PROOF mac   = HMAC-SHA-256(K_renew, "SR1 offer" || callId || ticket || challenge || nonce)
CONFIRM mac = HMAC-SHA-256(K_renew, "SR1 armed" || callId || ticket || challenge || nonce)
hop keys    = HKDF-SHA-256(IKM = K_renew, salt = challenge || nonce, info = "SR1 hop" || callId || ticket, L = 64)
              bytes 0-31: offerer -> armed, bytes 32-63: armed -> offerer
```

- The armed phone sends a fresh random `CHALLENGE` on every connection, as soon as the client's subscribe is accepted.
- A connection that has not delivered a valid `PROOF` within 1,000 ms of its challenge, or delivers a wrong one, is dropped at once, and the set keeps advertising (squatters). At most 8 connections are challenged per attempt.
- A valid `PROOF` consumes the ticket (single use). The armed phone then sends `CONFIRM`; the offerer drops a link whose `CONFIRM` is wrong (an advert copied from the air) and tries again while time remains.
- MACs are compared in constant time. No BMH2 or BMH3 handshake runs on the new link: only the two endpoints hold `K_renew`, and on a direct call the end-to-end peer is the hop peer, so the proofs pin exactly the old link's peer without putting an identity on the air.
- `BMS1` then runs on the new link with the hop keys: each direction from sequence 0, exact-next as always. The link also has its own chunk sequences from 0 and, with `GR1` on, its own `GR1` HELLO and history; nothing of the old link's `BMS1`, `GR1` or heartbeat state carries over.

**Sequence (offerer = the phone that stopped hearing its peer).**
1. *Trigger.* Renewal negotiated and in scope; the call `ACTIVE` for at least 10 s; Stage A's soft state (no record for 3 s while this phone's own writes still complete) has lasted 1 s; at most 2 attempts per call and 60 s between attempts. The offerer sends `OFFER` on the old link (its own direction works) and at once scans for the derived UUID, for at most 6 s.
2. *Armed phone.* On `OFFER` it checks scope and limits (else `REFUSED`), registers a GATT server and starts the advert, both within 3 s (else `REFUSED` server registration or advertising; a registration that finishes later is closed). Then it sends `ARMED` on the old link (best effort: that direction is the stalled one). From `OFFER` until `BIND` or the end of its window, it suspends its liveness hang-up, sends no liveness `HANG_UP`, and treats a failing or closed old link as part of the renewal.
3. *Break only after found.* The offerer keeps the old link until it sees the advert. Not found within 6 s: the old link stays and the call continues under the normal liveness rules. The peer heard again (any record other than `RENEW`) before the break: the renewal is abandoned and the offerer sends `REFUSED` abandoned. Found: the offerer stops its old link's writer (joined), closes the old link, connects as central, runs the opening and sends `BIND` as its first `BMS1` record on the new link. A failure before `CONFIRM` retries with a new sighting while time remains.
4. *Bind.* The armed phone accepts `BIND` only as the first `BMS1` record of the connection whose `PROOF` consumed that ticket, only once, and only with the current ticket. A `BIND` on the old link or on any other link is ignored and logged. After `BIND` it closes the old link; anything that still arrives there is dropped unopened, so no old-link record is processed after a new-link record.
5. *Re-assertion.* After `BIND` each side requests a key frame, sends its own next video as a key frame, and sends once its current microphone and camera signals and its own `LABELS` record if it sent one in this call. A record cut off with the old link is lost; the end-to-end opener already accepts increasing sequence gaps. A side that hangs up during a renewal stops the renewal; its peer then ends at its own bound.
6. *Bounds.* While it scans and connects, the offerer's renewal replaces its liveness close: it ends the call as link lost 12 s after it sent `OFFER` if no renewal was bound. The armed phone stops advertising 7 s after `OFFER` arrived; 12 s after it, without an accepted `BIND`, it ends the call as link lost when its old link failed or closed, and otherwise disarms and resumes its liveness rules (the offerer kept the old link). Server registration on a full registration table is inside these bounds.
7. *Collision.* A phone that receives `OFFER` while its own offer is pending answers `REFUSED` collision when it is the caller (role 0) and keeps its offer; the callee abandons its offer and arms.

**Downgrade and threats.** Bit 4 is inside the signed end-to-end hello, so stripping it only removes renewal (the call ends as before). No algorithm or key is negotiated: the hop keys come from the call's own end-to-end keys and fresh randomness from both sides. A third phone cannot complete the opening or bind (no `K_renew`); a phone that forwards the frames gains no key. A hostile peer can make a phone advertise at most twice per call for 7 s each. The renewal advert shows that a call is in progress near this phone, as the call's own link already does; its UUID is new for every attempt and links neither to the phone nor to earlier calls.

**Logs (Android).** `BENCHMARK call_renewal: role=offerer|armed|supervisor | phase=offer|scan|found|connecting|connect_timeout|proved|bound|abandoned|refused|yielded|link_dead|failed | reason= | ms= | attempt= | nonDiscoverable= | addressKind=` (`addressKind` from the advert address's two top bits: `rpa`, `static` or `other`; never the address), and `BENCHMARK call_renewal_silence: role= | ms=` (last record heard on the old link to the first record on the new one). With renewal negotiated, a per-call liveness tick (role `supervisor`) ends a call whose link died while no renewal owned its liveness, and a silence that a blocked writer did not end. The DEBUG hook `test_call_tx_stall_after_s=N` makes this phone's call writer stop sending on its first link N s after the call became `ACTIVE`, once per call, to induce a one-way stall on any pair.

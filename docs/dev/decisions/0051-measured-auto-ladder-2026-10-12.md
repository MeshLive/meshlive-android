# Measured Auto ladder (2026-10-12)

What was wrong. Auto started every session at 240p / 15 FPS / 200 kbps whatever the link measured. Display-referenced VMAF puts 360p15 8-9 points above 240p15 at the same rate from about 150 kbps; the call ladder never went above its start geometry, so a 240p start disabled every upward step; 200 kbps video needs a 324 kbps link; the picker allowed starved combinations such as 854x480 at 30 FPS and 200 kbps (bits per pixel per frame 0.019 against a fitted floor of 0.05-0.065); the 100 kbps picker minimum is above what a 65-110 kbps link carries with audio. The 2026-10-10 default rationale ("WebRTC VP9 360p start limit about 350 kbps") did not match the table in `CallResolutionLadder`: 640x360 is 190 kbps (VP9) and 256 kbps (AV1); 350 is the 960x540 entry.

What the evidence says (confidence in brackets).

- Content swings VMAF more than codec or encoder (the same encode scores 94-97 on a camera pan, 89.5 on standard stress, 85.3 on ReadySetGo, 52-91 on the synthetic coverage mosaic at 110-840 kbps), so a rung is a start rate and the controller's feedback moves it [high].
- Quality grows about log(bitrate): +11 to +14 VMAF per doubling between 100 and 800 kbps [high]. Equal-quality bitrate scales with pixels^0.3-0.5 and fps^0.5-0.6 (the Forasoft model used 0.85 for both) [medium-high].
- 360p beats 240p at every tested rate from 150 kbps up (AV1 +8.8 VMAF at 250, VP9 +8.2); 480p overtakes 360p near 330 kbps (VP9 software) and 430-490 kbps (AV1) [medium-high, display-referenced hard content, software encoders]. 720p15 AV1 gains 3-4 more only on S24-class encode margin (1.82x; S22 1.05-1.19x warm; M31 and A15s none) [medium].
- Halving fps from 30 to 15 gains +2.3 (AV1 360p) to +13.5 (VP9 480p) at equal rate; 480p to 360p at 15 FPS gains only +1.2 to +3.9. So fps first above 15, resolution first below it [medium-high].
- The shipped hardware VP9 needs 1.7-2.3x the Forasoft rate at 640x480 for VMAF 80 on hard content; AV1 software saves only 20-35% against it, not the 40% of the Forasoft ratio [medium].
- Wire model `1.076 x (video + 0.64 x fps) + audio wire` (audio wire 50 kbps for Opus 32, 34 for Opus 16) is within 8% of two clean HCI-snoop points [medium, two points].

30 FPS needs 930 (AV1) / 1190 (VP9) kbps of video at 480p. 360p24 is a motion alternative and is not selected by the quality ladder. A weak AV1 CPU uses the VP9 thresholds (3.5-5.6 VMAF lost at lower cpu-used).

Not measured (do not read as a pass): nothing under 100 kbps of video, no 10-12 FPS rung, no live data at 426x240 or 854x480 (352x288 and 640x480 stand in), no real Camera2 or talking-head content, no VMAF p5 or media age on the live runs, hardware VP9 crossovers assumed equal to software VP9, the encode-margin store that would classify a phone as strong does not exist yet (DEBUG class override only).

What was built, behind `test_auto_ladder` (default off, DEBUG launch extra only; see SWITCHES). Pure policy `AutoQualityLadder` (rungs, wire model, plan, thresholds, picker guard) with the existing adaptation moving along the same rungs:

- Start: the plan for the measured link (unmeasured: 300 kbps goodput, so 360p15; a call direction gets half the measured link). The request becomes a climb bound, so it is not a fixed ceiling in Auto (a call: the transport maximum; a stream: the learned fan-out budget for one link, at most the transport maximum, while fan-out admission keeps planning at the start rate so a source admits as many children as a fixed-rate plan would); `LinkRateController` starts at the plan's rate and climbs only on clean windows, backing off on backlog or late children. The call header is the maximum geometry for the class (480p; 720p for a strong AV1 encoder) and the ladder starts at the plan's rung inside it, which is what lets Auto go above the start rung.
- Moving: `CallResolutionLadder` with the measured limits (AV1 150 / VP9 175 kbps for 360p replace WebRTC's 256 / 190; 240p is the floor), one rung up after the target holds 1.25x the next rung's need for 10 s and 15 s since the last change, down after 1.5 s below the current rung's need. Frame rate follows `AutoQualityLadder.fpsForVideoKbps` (24/30 only while their rung's need is met, then 15, then 12 and 10 below the 240p15 need). Streams use the same ladder and restart their camera encoder at a change; they are skipped while paused or while a restart is in progress, and follow the weakest direct child, so a source with children never outruns its tightest child. Relays handle their own downstream hop (hop-local rule).
- Manual: Advanced values are never changed. With the switch on the picker shows a warning when bits per pixel per frame (640x480 equivalent, pixel exponent 0.4) fall below the fitted floor (AV1 0.050, VP9 0.065; "too low" under half of it). Blocking Start for a starved combination is not implemented; whether to do so is an open product decision.
- Logged: `BENCHMARK auto_ladder: scope=call|call_start|stream|stream_resolution|stream_fps|call_fps ...` for every plan and rung change, plus the existing `call_resolution` lines.

First phone A/B (stream ASUS to S24 over GATT, live Camera2 dark scene; control 640x480@15 at a 300 kbps request, viewer 0 freezes; Auto arm 4 freezes, 5.1% frozen, longest 2.07 s at the same wire rate). Findings and fixes (2026-10-13):

- The planned 790 kbps never ran because the link estimate was stale-high. The plan used the 1074 kbps probe (the same value as the transport prior; nothing proven), but the link ran at 1M PHY (the adaptive PHY memory had moved the pair off 2M) and the video it carried was about 150-180 kbps (about 245 kbps total with audio, against the 131-263 kbps one- and two-PDU bounds the log gives for the 15 ms connection interval at 1M). The encoder was at 790 kbps only for the first 0.4 s; the rate controller then started at the peer's remembered 300 kbps, most likely recorded by the control arm's clean windows at its 300 kbps target (inferred; the stored value was not read). Open: the plan still trusts the probe for the start rung and the unknown-peer start, so a fresh pair with a stale-high probe starts too high; the safer estimate (delivery reports, PHY-aware) is not built.
- "/1037" in the log is the request used as a climb bound (the learned fan-out budget for one link), not a rate.
- 720x480 was chosen because the plan's 854x480 header was mapped to the largest camera mode under it by pixels. The ASUS offers 176x144, 320x240, 352x288, 640x480, 720x480 and 1280x720: 720x480 is the only 3:2 mode, so the ladder logged `no_smaller_rung` and the stream stayed at 720x480 while its target fell to 91 kbps. Fixed: `AutoQualityLadder.fitSettings` takes the mode from the aspect-ratio group that has at least two ladder sizes (prefers the requested aspect), which gives 640x480 and 320x240 here; start limits are judged per size with the measured 0.4 pixel exponent (640x480 needs about 294 kbps VP9 / 383 AV1, not the 330 / 430 of 854x480).
- The freezes were rate-control overshoot, not a camera restart or a keyframe (no rung change happened; the keyframe was 247 bytes): the climb step is a tenth of the request, so with a 1037 kbps bound one clean step was 104 kbps (300 to 403 kbps, +34%) on a link that carried about 170. The no-loss queue grew from 0 to 4.9 s, the viewer froze (4 events, the longest 2.07 s, all in the first 20 s), and the cuts cascaded 403, 235, 117, 91 kbps while the software VP9 encoder overshot each new target by 26-100%. The control cut once (300 to 120) and never climbed (step 30). Fixed: with the Auto switch the step is a tenth of the current target (never more than the old step), so the climb is as small as the control's.
- Not fixed, for the next A/B: the encoder overshoot at low targets (software VP9 at 720x480 or 640x480 sent 297 kbps at a 235 kbps target) and the first-window overshoot at the remembered 300 kbps are shared with the control arm and need their own comparison.

Evidence start (follow-up to the first A/B, 2026-10-13). The 1074 kbps "probe" is the child's arrival-train rate (`LinkCapacity.recordProbe` takes `report.arrivalKbps`), a burst rate that already overstated shared GATT links, not goodput; the per-peer link value is the same measurement. Under the switch the start therefore rests on `AutoQualityLadder.evidencePlan`, evaluated when the first viewer is authenticated and before the encoder exists (so there is no restart cost):

- link = the lower of the probe and, for a peer the phone remembers as bad-2M (`AdaptivePhyMemory`), the 1M ceiling `2 PDUs x 247 B x 8 / interval` (263 kbps at the 15 ms high-priority interval; measured: the A/B pair carried about 245 kbps in total on 1M at 15 ms, 1.9x the one-PDU bound); the interval is assumed 15 ms because the source does not hold the peer's address (the controller climbs if it is shorter);
- delivered evidence caps the video: the rate each authenticated peer's link last carried cleanly for streams (weakest peer; any peer without one counts as none), the rung dropping until it fits;
- (superseded by the cold start below) a probe alone, with no PHY bound and nothing delivered, started one rung down at no more than 1.5x that rung's entry need (VP9 probe 1074: 360p15 at 260 kbps instead of 480p15 at 790); an assumed link keeps the approved 360p15; calls with no remembered peer rate take the same probe-only rule on half the probe;
- the rate controller starts at that value for every peer (a remembered rate can lower it but not raise it past the evidence), steps up a tenth of the target only after windows that carried at least 80% of target plus audio (the usual rule is 60%), and remembers what those clean windows carried, not the target.

First-window overshoot at the remembered start: both arms of the A/B started at the remembered 300 kbps while the link carried about 170 (video encoder 337 and 328 kbps in the first full second; the first cut came 7-8 s after the start in both). The remembered 300 was a target the dark-scene encoder had not filled in an earlier run; with the 80% fill rule and the carried-rate memory the next start is what was demonstrated, and the evidence cap keeps a stale remembered value from starting above the PHY bound. Expected start for the A/B pair: ASUS to S24 GATT on 1M, VP9 software: 240p15 at 150 kbps (AV1: 360p15 at 150), then a climb of about 10% per three loaded clean windows; without the ASUS bad-2M memory it starts 360p15 at 260 kbps. Untested on phones; the encoder overshoot at low targets and the interval assumption are unmeasured.

Weak-pair phone A/B (calls over GATT, A15s and M31, build 0d7e07b5, six runs per arm and direction, 2026-10-13). A15s to M31 video: control 10 fps, 0.8 freezes (0.4% frozen, longest 324 ms, playout 250 ms); Auto 9.7 fps, 12 freezes (18.5% frozen, longest 2.4-3.8 s), playout 600 ms (+350). M31 to A15s: fps and freezes equal, playout 594 against 250 ms and held age up to 786 against 250 ms. Findings and fixes (all under the Auto switch; the controller, header and rung fixes of the two earlier A/B follow-ups were not in that build):

- A15s class and header. The libaom encoder was classed STANDARD although its own record said encode load 1.5-1.9 (p95 about 100 ms for a 720x480 frame, 291 ms per megapixel; 15 FPS at 640x360 is already load 1.0 and only 240p-class sizes fit). The header was the lone 3:2 mode 720x480, so the ladder was inactive (`no_smaller_rung`); the control used the manual 352x288 and held 15 FPS. Fixed: `EncoderBurstProfileStore` records the p95 encode time per megapixel for every software session, `AutoQualityLadder.encodeLoad` predicts the load of any size and frame rate, `encoderClassFromLoad` makes a CPU-bound encoder WEAK (VP9 thresholds) or a fast one STRONG (the missing encode-margin store), and `usableRungs` / `fitSettings` offer only sizes at or under a load of 0.8 (the lowest rung stays when none fits). For the A15s that leaves 240p10-15 and the camera mode 352x288; a phone with no record yet is unfiltered until its first session writes one (the first control call does).
- Frame rate flips. `call_fps` flipped 10 and 15 FPS 6-11 times a minute: the target jumped between 40 kbps (cut to the floor on a 63 kbps delivered link) and 150 kbps (the old climb step, a tenth of the 1100 kbps request, 110 kbps; fixed by the small climb step) and the curve followed it. Fixed: `FpsHysteresis` takes a lower rate after 2 s of asking, a higher one only after 10 s and 15 s since the last change, one level at a time. A software encoder over its frame budget (live p95 encode time over the interval above 1.0 for 3 s) now steps the picture one rung down (`CallResolutionLadder.onEncoderLoad`, reason `encoder_load`) and blocks the way back up for 60 s, for calls and streams.
- Playout 600 ms. 600 ms is the call adaptive maximum: the clock grows by each late arrival's lateness plus 50 ms and shrinks only after 10 s without a late frame. The benchmark launches ran with speech-led playout off (`call_policy: speechLedPlayout=false`); user launches on GATT run with it on, which caps what late video can add at 150 ms. The late video came from the sender: M31 started at a remembered 571 kbps (a target remembered by an earlier build), its first keyframe plus the 340-520 kbps climbs left the A15s link 'peer late' with 546-1014 ms of peer queue and late p95 345-1676 ms, and the receiver stayed pinned. Fixed at the cause: a remembered call rate can no longer start above the evidence start for an unknown peer (`autoCallMemory`, one rung down from the probe), the climb is a tenth of the target, and remembered rates are what the clean windows carried. The ladder does not change the playout policy: the rerun holds it equal in both arms (`test_call_speech_led_playout=true` and `test_call_video_fragments=true`, the user GATT defaults, which benchmark launches do not apply on their own).
- Sender queue drops. The A15s call video queue dropped 34, 136 and 68 frames in three of six Auto runs (none in the control): its uplink delivered about 63 kbps (peer report) while audio alone is 32 kbps payload plus framing, so even the 40 kbps floor overruns it. Fixed by audio first: when the controller sits at its lowest video rate and the link is still behind for 6 s, `AudioFirstGate` pauses the call video on the camera-off path (the peer sees a camera-off call; log `scope=call_audio_first`), and tries again after 30 s, doubling to 120 s when the resumed video is paused again within 20 s.
- Not fixed: the probe is still the starting estimate for a pair with no remembered rate (it was 367 kbps on a link delivering 63); the encode-time record is per encoder, written at the session's settled preset; hardware encoders have no load signal; the audio-first pause is a camera-off, not a lower picture, so a pair whose uplink carries 60-100 kbps gets audio only.

Raw source geometry and the latency guard (2026-10-13, lane follow-ups). Raw fixtures: the green and pink corruption on the S24 viewer was a harness/source bug, not the codec. The ASUS compat VP9 path mapped the 640x360 request to its nearest camera mode 640x480 (`refreshVideoModes`), and the 640x360 fixture (300 frames, 103,680,000 bytes) read at 640x480 is exactly 225 frames, so the whole-frame size check passed and every frame was misaligned. Now the declared geometry is the runner's `test_raw_width` and `test_raw_height` (else the launch's requested size before any mapping); the encoder refuses (visible error and `BENCHMARK raw_source: status=REFUSED | declared= | encoder= | reason=`) unless the file is a whole number of declared frames and the encoder runs at the declared size, and camera modes no longer constrain a raw source's geometry or frame rate. Nothing is rescaled or reinterpreted. Every camera mode mapping is logged as `BENCHMARK mode_mapping: requested= effective= | reason=nearest_camera_mode`. The Auto ladder's mode fitting never ran for raw sources (`autoLadderActive` is false for them); the mapping was the older nearest-mode snap.

Latency guard (lane 2: an M31 stream source planned 480p24 at 870 kbps on a 1185 kbps link, the frame rate rose to about 23, the sender queue reached 1.86 s and the receiver's playout delay its 2000 ms cap, 1.2 s above the control): a rise of the frame rate or of the picture size is allowed only while the sender queue plus the media queued at the peer stayed within 150 ms and no child reported its playout delay rising for 5 s (`ClimbGuard`, fed by every sample of every child, so the tightest child decides); congestion (400 ms queued, or late with a queue present) lowers the frame rate one level at once and holds rises for 30 s; steps down are never gated.

Before delivered evidence (Camera2 A/B on build e918ce96, ASUS to S24 GATT): the first plan came from the plain plan, 480p24 at 1100 kbps from a 1488 kbps probe, and the stream header kept 24 FPS; the viewer froze 12 times (7.3% frozen), while the same pair warmed by a delivered 124 kbps planned 240p15 with none. The arm-time start and the header now use the probe-only rule for streams and calls: one rung down, at most 1.5x that rung's entry need, and 15 FPS at most (`AutoQualityLadder.MAX_UNCONFIRMED_FPS`; a PHY-bound start is capped the same way); 24 and 30 FPS need delivered evidence, which exists only after a peer is known, so today's headers stay at 15 FPS. The runner's TUNABLES carry `auto_ladder` and `auto_ladder_class`.

PHY transient and the climb (lane 4, build 326aed2c, ASUS to A15s GATT, cold arm against the fixed 640x480/300 kbps control, 2026-10-13). Auto's A15s viewer froze 3.8-5.6% against 0% for the control, with a peak sender queue of 4.2-5.9 s against 1.6 s. The arms did not meet the same link:

- The control connected on 1M from the first byte (`adaptive_phy ... reason=remembered at=connect`, the bad-2M mark an earlier run had left), while the cold arm ran after `test_reset_learned_state`, connected on 2M, and stayed there until `adaptive_phy: from=2M to=1M reason=one_pdu_per_event (kbps=85 blocked=0.87)` at 10 s. On 2M this A15s pair carries one PDU per connection event: about 85 kbps in total, less than the 40 kbps floor plus 32 kbps audio plus framing, so a queue of 881, 1562, 2238, 3453, 3926, 4206 ms grew at the floor and nothing a rate controller can do changes that; the cut to the floor at 3 s (0.85 x 84 - 32 = 39 kbps) was right. After the move to 1M the link carried 105-135 kbps in total, the queue drained to 0 within 5 s, and the controller then climbed 10 kbps per three clean windows.
- The control's "300 kbps" was a request cap, not a rate: with 640x480 and a dark scene the encoder produced 117-162 kbps (its target went 120, 150, 180, 120) and the sender held a standing 1.3-1.4 s queue for the whole 90 s at 154-179 kbps TX. The cold arm's climb reached 146 kbps by 52 s (TX 130 kbps, queue 0 to 200 ms) and was cut back to 94 only when it passed the link's ~130 kbps at 56 s. So the sustainable video rate on this pair is about 100-150 kbps, which Auto reached; the control got there with a longer queue and no freezes only because it began on 1M.
- Fixed: a PHY change is now a link change. `AdaptivePhyLink.onPhy` counts a real change of the transmit PHY (`PhyChangeTracker`, `LinkChangeEvents.epoch`, log `BENCHMARK link_change`); the Auto stream and call controllers see the new epoch at their next sample and `restartAfterLinkChange` raises a target that the old link had forced down to 60% of the evidence start (84 kbps for the 140 kbps cold start), clears the climb and ceiling state, and blocks a cut for 3 s so the queue left by the old link can drain (queue age is kept, it is real). It never lowers a target. The Auto climb step has a 20 kbps floor (10 before): from the floor, 40 to 100 kbps took 30 s. With both, the replay of this run is at 164 kbps 12 s after the move against 120 for the old climb.
- Not changed: the adaptive PHY policy itself (one warm-up window plus three 2 s windows, about 8 s after the link saturates) is shared and tuned, and it is what bounds the peak queue on a 2M link like this; shortening it under Auto is the lever for the 4-6 s peak and needs its own A/B. The comparison needs both arms from the same PHY state: either reset the learned state before the control too, or leave the bad-2M mark in place for both.

Cold start on a weak link (lane 4, build d8c7589c, ASUS to A15s GATT, 2026-10-13): a probe of 1488 kbps planned 480p15 at 495 kbps, the link carried 44-90 kbps, the sender queue reached 9 s, three cuts took the target to 40 kbps and 320x240 within 15 s, and the viewer was frozen 62% of 17 s. The probe is the child's arrival-train rate, a burst figure that can overstate a weak link 10-20x, and the earlier probe-only rule (one rung down, 1.5x the entry need: 360p15 at 260 kbps or 480p15 at 495 kbps) still offered 3-7x what such a link carries. A cold start (a probe alone: no remembered rate, no PHY bound) is now 30% of the plain plan's video, at most 140 kbps (just under the 360p entry of both codecs, so the ladder starts at 240p), 240p-class sizes and 15 FPS at most (`PROBE_ONLY_VIDEO_FRACTION`, `PROBE_ONLY_MAX_VIDEO_KBPS`, `PROBE_ONLY_MAX_PIXELS`); a probe too small to carry video stays audio only. The offered load on a 70 kbps link drops from about 17 s of queue per second to about 2 s per second, and the rate controller's first sample cuts to the 40 kbps floor. The climb that follows is the controller's (10% of the target per three loaded clean windows), then the stream ladder's 30 s and 60 s timers for size, so a strong link reaches 360p after about a minute rather than at once. The warm paths are unchanged: a remembered delivered rate, a PHY-bound start, and the assumed link (360p15) plan as before. Not done: the carried rate of the first one or two seconds (audio and handshake) as a pre-video probe, because the authenticated hop has no throughput signal before video flows; the ASUS and A15s use software encoders (`reason=yuv_input_encoder`), so the GPU scaler cannot be phone-tested on them.

Stability follow-up (second Camera2 A/B, build 47300560, ASUS to S24 GATT, 2026-10-13). The viewer's decoder rebuild worked (`decoder_rebuild reason=size`, 66-112 ms, no errors), but the cold arm changed size four times in 40 s and each change cost about 1.7 s of "Recovering video".

- Why the target fell to 97 kbps. It was not an under-filled encoder. At the 495-544 kbps target the encoder produced 519-555 kbps, the sender queue grew 616, 1320, 2015, 2754, 3278, 3816 ms with the child reporting late, and the link carried only 152-212 kbit per window ("BLE GATT TX 262-323 kbps", about the 263 kbps two-PDU bound of a 15 ms interval); the cut to 0.85 x 152 - 32 = 97 kbps was the rate controller's usual response to a 3.8 s queue. The probe (1488 kbps) was a burst rate; the same pair in the warm arm carried 700-800 kbps on the same 15 ms interval, so the link capacity moved by a factor of three between adjacent runs for a reason the logs do not show. What the ladder did wrong was to turn that into size changes: it follows the target alone, with 10 s up holds and 15 s between changes.
- Stream resolution changes are now rare (streams only; calls keep their timers). A step down needs the target under the rung's limit for 3 s and congestion evidence that has lasted 3 s (`ClimbGuard.sustainedCongestion`: queue of 400 ms or more, or a late child with a queue, without a clean sample in between), so a target that fell on its own never changes the size; at least 15 s between changes. A step up needs 30 s without a queue over 150 ms or a late child, the target at 1.25x the larger rung for 30 s, and 60 s since the last change, one rung at a time. Replaying arm B with these timers gives one change (480p to 240p after 3 s of congestion) and no step back before 60 s.
- Arm C's frame-rate steps (15 to 12 to 10 at a 705 kbps TX, no freezes) came from the earlier congestion rule of the latency guard: a 534-580 ms queue from the controller's own climb overshoot (offered 1003 kbit against 800 carried, child on time) stepped the frame rate one level at once. Queue age alone is the rate controller's job and it had already cut the target; the frame rate now steps on congestion only when it is severe and continuous (1000 ms or more queued, or a late child with 400 ms, for 4 s).
- The viewer keeps the last frame through a planned size change: a keyframe the sender marked FLAG_DISCONTINUITY (the restart marker, set only when the restart changes the coded size) no longer starts the recovery cover (`isPlannedSizeChange`, log `planned_size_change: keepLastFrame=true`); a recovery already showing keeps its cover, and an unmarked rebuild is still treated as a recovery. The picture stays as the old size's last frame until the first frame of the new size renders.

Risks to check on phones. A resolution change restarts the camera encoder (call and now stream) and its first keyframe is large on a weak link; receivers must follow the in-band size change inside the header geometry, including relay children that joined before the change; AV1 repeats a codec-config packet per rung, and the broadcaster keeps up to its cap of distinct configs for late joiners. The measured ladder's 300 kbps unmeasured assumption can start 360p15 on a pair that carries less; the controller then walks it down within seconds.

Phone A/B plan (control = switch absent; arm = `test_auto_ladder=true`, same frozen APK, same pair, same transport, rate control Automatic; `test_auto_ladder_class=strong` only on the S24). Weak pair (65-150 kbps goodput, e.g. ASUS to A15s GATT, or Wi-Fi 2.4 GHz on): call and stream, expect audio-only recommendation or 240p10-15, no freeze longer than the control, media age not above control. Mid pair (300-450): call and stream at 360p15 against 240p/200; expect VMAF +8-9 at equal rate. Strong pair (700-1000, e.g. ASUS to S24 GATT): call and stream, 480p15/24 against 240p/200; strong class 720p15. Measure per case: VMAF (display reference where the source is native raw), delivered fps and visible-fps intervals, freezes and longest stall, receiver playout/media age lines, every rung change (`auto_ladder`, `call_resolution`), requested versus effective, camera restarts, and that Manual launches are byte-identical to the control.

## Viewer decoder across a size change (2026-10-13)

Changed: `VideoStreamDecoder.kt`, new `DecoderFormatTracker.kt` and `CodedVideoSize.kt`, `BitstreamDroppability.kt` (the AV1 sequence header now keeps `max_frame_width/height`), `AndroidVideoReceiver.kt`, `PrivateCallMedia.kt` (follow the new size; a replacement call decoder starts from the current csd), `MainActivity.kt` (marker on `restartStreamCamera` and `startPrivateCallCamera`, control extra), `CameraVideoEncoder.kt` (`outputResolution`), `BluetoothVideoBroadcaster.kt` and `ForwardedStreamSecurity.kt` (late-joiner csd cache), `DebugTunables.kt`. Wire: none (PROTOCOL "Mid-stream size changes"). Switch: default-on `test_decoder_size_rebuild` (control arm false).

What was broken (offline review of the code against libwebrtc, media3 and the MediaCodec reference; the corruption itself is not yet reproduced on a phone): the viewer configured one MediaCodec at the header's size, with no `KEY_MAX_WIDTH/HEIGHT`, and a size change was only logged (`decoder_output_size`). Under the Auto ladder the header names the requested (maximum) size while the encoder starts at the ladder's rung, so a viewer could be misconfigured from its first frame; a rung change restarts the encoder and its new csd was queued as a separate `BUFFER_FLAG_CODEC_CONFIG` buffer although MediaCodec accepts a changed configuration only together with a keyframe, and only for adaptive VP8/VP9/H.264/H.265 (AV1 is not documented). Recovery after a flush re-queued the stream-start csd, so after a rung change it put the old AV1 sequence header in front of a keyframe of the new size. The restart sent no discontinuity, so frames of the old size could still be queued ahead of the new keyframe. A late joiner could be handed the csd of two encoders (up to 4 cached packets at a source, 8 at a relay).

Fix: libwebrtc's `AndroidVideoDecoder.reinitDecode` pattern (release and create a decoder for the new picture; media3 re-creates a non-adaptive decoder the same way). The size is read from the keyframe bitstream so old and new builds stay wire-compatible; a keyframe that does not say changes nothing. The first decoder is configured at its first keyframe's size with the header as the adaptive-playback maximum. A size-changing restart marks its first keyframe `FLAG_DISCONTINUITY` (old receivers treat it as a recovery point); restarts that keep the size (flip, camera on/off) are unmarked and behave as before unless their csd differs. If the new decoder cannot be opened with the new csd it is retried without csd. Alternatives not built: in-place adaptive reconfiguration (rejected: undocumented for AV1, and the vendor results differ), a dedicated size record (rejected: it would break older receivers).

Rejected/limits: a relay sees only outer mesh flags, so its late-joiner csd cache is pruned by "config after media" only; a source also prunes at a discontinuity keyframe that follows no new config.

Re-check (affected scope): every Auto-ladder stream case and call-ladder case, any pair, any transport, VP9 and AV1, hardware and software decoders, plus a preservation chain for relayed streams and late joiners. Cases with the ladder off and no restart are affected only by the first-keyframe configure size and `KEY_MAX_*` (identical sizes when the header equals the keyframe size).

Phone test plan (frozen APK; control arm `test_decoder_size_rebuild=false` on the same pair): (1) stream rung change down and up (Auto ladder on the weak pair, VP9 hardware viewer and AV1 software viewer, plus an AV1 hardware viewer): `decoder_rebuild reason=size` or `size+csd` once per change, no green or pink frame, first frame at the new size within about 300 ms of the keyframe, display aspect follows; (2) camera flip and camera off/on at a constant size: no `decoder_rebuild` unless csd changed, rendering unchanged; (3) a viewer joining after a rung change (direct and through a relay): configured at the current size (`decoder_format configured=`), csd count 1; (4) decoder recovery (a forced key-frame loss) after a rung change: the restored csd is the new one (`Decoder recovery restored 1 codec-config buffer(s)`) and the picture recovers; (5) a call resolution-ladder step in both directions; (6) `test_decoder_size_rebuild=false` reproduces the earlier behaviour (control). Pass per phone: no corrupt picture across the change, rebuild under 150 ms on hardware decoders (`decoder_rebuild ms=`), no new freeze beyond the one keyframe wait.

## GPU scale ladder (2026-10-13)

Changed: new `GpuScaleMath.kt` (rung sizes, crop, texture matrix, capture-mode choice, the switch `GpuScaleFeature`), `GpuFrameRenderer.kt` (EGL context, external-texture shader, one draw per frame), `GpuScaleCapture.kt` (the shared camera: open once, encoders attach and detach); `CameraVideoEncoder.kt` (new `gpuScale` request, `retainCaptureForRestart`, per-rung BENCHMARK lines), `DeviceVideoCapabilities.kt` (`gpuModesFor`), `MainActivity.kt` (stream and call fit, ladder rungs, camera-flip check, restart keep-alive, resets), `AndroidPrivateVideoCall.kt` (`supportedVideoModes` now receives the requested settings, so the call's negotiated size is checked against the GPU rungs and not replaced by the nearest camera mode). Time: 2026-10-13. Wire: none.

Why: a rung change restarted the camera at another camera mode (hundreds of milliseconds to over a second, with a "Camera could not restart" failure mode), and the rungs were limited to each phone's mode list (the ASUS 3:2 mode after 16:9 rungs changed the aspect). libwebrtc captures at one size and draws every frame into the encoder's input surface through a crop/scale matrix (`VideoAdapter` rungs are exact fractions of the capture, a few pixels cropped for alignment instead of distorting; `HardwareVideoEncoder` re-creates the codec on a size change and its first frame is a keyframe); Grafika's `ContinuousCaptureActivity` is the minimal form of the same pattern. MediaCodec has no runtime size parameter, so the encoder reset is unavoidable, but it is now tens of milliseconds and touches neither the camera nor the aspect.

Behaviour (default off, `test_gpu_scale_ladder=true` together with `test_auto_ladder=true`, DEBUG launches only):
- Applies to a stream or call that runs the Auto ladder on a hardware surface-input encoder (`GpuScaleMath.offReason` names the first blocking condition in the `scope=stream|call` BENCHMARK line). It does not apply to the native libaom (YUV input), raw fixtures, benchmark reference frames or Manual video settings; they keep the camera-mode path and their current behaviour.
- Capture: the smallest camera mode (SurfaceTexture output sizes, at most 1920x1088, at the session frame rate) whose same-aspect picture covers the session's largest picture, else the smallest mode whose centre crop to the session aspect does. The top rung is then a 1:1 copy and every other rung a downscale, never an upscale.
- Rungs: 720/540/480/360/240/180/144p at the session aspect (16:9 snaps from 854x480, so 240p is 426x240 as before), width and height rounded to the encoder's `getWidthAlignment`/`getHeightAlignment`, only sizes `isSizeSupported` accepts and the capture can supply without upscaling. `DeviceVideoCapabilities.gpuModesFor` returns them as modes so `AutoQualityLadder.fitSettings`, `CallResolutionLadder.buildRungs` and the flip check are unchanged.
- Draw: one external-texture draw per frame; the crop is centred and exactly the rung's aspect; a downscale of 1.05x or more averages four bilinear taps a quarter of an output pixel around the centre (a box-like filter, so 720p to 144p is not an aliased single tap); a 1:1 copy is one tap. `eglPresentationTimeANDROID` carries the camera's sensor timestamp, so presentation times stay continuous across encoder resets.
- Switching: `old.close()` (the capture stays open 2.5 s when the close is a rung change, and is released at once for camera off, flip, pause and stop), then `new CameraVideoEncoder.start()` on the same executor; the old encoder is closed first because a phone may allow only one hardware encoder of a codec. The restart marker (`RestartDiscontinuityMarker`) and the viewer's decoder rebuild are unchanged.
- Orientation: Camera2 buffers stay in sensor orientation, as when the camera fed the encoder directly (display rotation is a flag, and the encoded front-camera stream is not mirrored: the viewer's transform logs mirror=false), so the GL path adds no rotation or mirror; the sensor timestamp base is the same as before.

Not done / limits: the native libaom path still takes camera-mode sizes (a GPU rung there needs a read-back to I420 or a CPU downscale; keyframe-free libaom downscale is a separate change); rungs below 240p exist in the table but the Auto limits (`callLimits`, floor 240p, the frame rate falls instead) keep them unreachable until a limit is measured at 65-150 kbps; a lens flip to a lens whose camera offers no suitable capture stops the stream with the usual message; the capture adds one GPU draw per frame and a YUV to RGB to YUV round trip in the encoder input (compare colour and VMAF at equal rate before promoting).

Re-check (affected scope when the switch is on): every Auto-ladder stream and call case on a hardware surface-input encoder (VP9 and AV1, any pair, any transport), relay and late-joiner chains that see a rung change, and camera flip, camera off/on and pause/resume of such sessions. With the switch off the code path is unchanged.

Phone test plan (frozen APK; control arm = `test_gpu_scale_ladder` absent with `test_auto_ladder=true`; same pair, transport, rate control Automatic; run per phone S24, ASUS, M31 VP9 hardware, and the A15s only to confirm it stays on the camera-mode path with `reason=yuv_input_encoder`): (1) stream and call rung changes down and up during a run (weak link to force the steps): `rung=WxH | encoderResetMs` per change (target tens of milliseconds, record the actual), `firstFrameMs`, no `Camera could not restart`, the preview never blinks, `decoder_rebuild reason=size` once per change on the viewer and no green or pink frame; (2) `gpuMs/frame` submit and finish p50/p95 per rung on each GPU (record, do not assume 1-2 ms), plus battery and thermal status over a long run against the control; (3) picture correctness with a controlled target: no stretch (aspect of every rung), correct rotation in portrait and landscape, back and front camera, lens flip mid-stream, camera off/on, pause/resume, and the 3:2 ASUS mode keeping 16:9 rungs through a crop; (4) colour and quality at equal rate against the control (the YUV to RGB round trip); (5) a viewer that joins after a rung change, direct and through a relay; (6) context loss and app background/foreground (the preview surface refresh).

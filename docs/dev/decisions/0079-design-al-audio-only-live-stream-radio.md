# Design AL: audio-only live stream (radio)

**Design review required.** Design record; implemented 2026-10-12 and on by default since 2026-10-17 (`test_audio_live=false` is the off arm; there is no Settings switch, user request). Nothing here changes BMH2/BMH3, BMS1, BME1, BTV3, BFG1, existing video streams or calls. The wire rules are in [protocol reference](../protocol/README.md) "Experimental audio-only live streams". Decision 2026-10-12 (user): compatibility with 0.2.0-beta matters little (few downloads, and the app has an updater), so where the only reason for a weaker design is that release, the cleaner design is used. The fail-safe rule stays: an older phone must never crash or show a black or broken picture. It simply cannot join an audio-only stream.

**Goal.** A standalone audio broadcast ("Go live: audio only", a nearby radio): announcements, events, emergencies, a neighbourhood or festival channel. It is a first-class stream kind, not "video with the camera off". Audio costs about one seventh of the lowest video setting on the wire, so it reaches more hops and more listeners, works on links where video collapses, and lets the screen stay off.

**What users get.**

- **Go live** (feature on) opens a short choice: *Video and audio* (today's flow) or *Audio only (radio)*. The choice is remembered. Audio only shows no camera preview: a panel with the title field (if Names and titles is on), a quality row (Voice, Low data, Music), a live input level meter, the listener count, and Start, Mute, Pause and Stop. Mute keeps the stream and its cadence running: it gates capture atomically, discards every captured sample and unsent partial batch (nothing recorded before the tap goes out afterwards, review 16), then sends signed silence; audio already sent may still play at listeners, and the panel says so; Pause releases the microphone (the lifecycle state PAUSED that streams already have), so the Android microphone indicator goes off.
- **Watch** rows for audio-only streams carry a radio icon and the subtitle "Audio only", then the usual reach ("nearby", "via 2 phones") and the paused and full labels. The row title is the signed or unconfirmed title, else "Radio from <name or handle>". Rows for blocked people stay hidden, as for video.
- **Listening screen** (no video area): the title or handle, a route chip ("direct" or "via N phones"), the live state (Live, Reconnecting, Paused by broadcaster, Ended), a moving level meter so a listener can tell audio is flowing, elapsed time, Mute and Stop. Volume is the phone's media volume; the app never changes it.
- **Background and lock screen.** Listening keeps going with the screen off and the app in the background. It runs under the existing foreground service (types `connectedDevice | mediaPlayback`) with a media-style notification and a framework `MediaSession` (no new library): actions Mute/Unmute (local only) and Stop; headset play/pause toggles local mute. The lock screen shows only neutral text ("Listening to a live broadcast"), a new `NotificationKind` beside the existing session kind (whose public text is just "Active"); it never shows a name (AL-9). The `MediaSession` carries no title or metadata, because lock-screen media controls, the quick-settings carousel, car and watch displays ignore the notification's public version; it is deactivated and released when listening ends. The notification uses the framework `Notification.MediaStyle` (the service uses `NotificationCompat` and androidx.media is not a dependency). The receiver stays owned by the activity in v1, so the service stops and clears its notification whenever the receiver closes (including activity destruction), and never says "Listening" without a running receiver (AL-10); an application-scoped listener is the follow-up if tests show activity kills.
- **Broadcaster safeguards (AL-3, AL-8).** A broadcaster uses `microphone | connectedDevice`, needs only the microphone and Nearby permissions (never the camera, unlike today's `hasRequiredPermissions()`), and must start from the visible app (Android 14 refuses the microphone type from the background). If the service ends up holding `connectedDevice` only (the existing refusal fallback), the broadcast stops with a visible error: a background `AudioRecord` without the microphone type delivers silence, and a silent "live" broadcast is not allowed. The broadcaster's notification says "Broadcasting audio" with Mute and Stop actions. Mute keeps the microphone open, so the Android indicator stays on, and the panel says so. A "Still broadcasting" reminder fires after 30 minutes with no listener or after 2 hours in total, and the broadcast stops 5 minutes after an unanswered reminder. An incoming private call during a broadcast: the call screen ends the broadcast after the user accepts (today's behaviour stops streaming when the call browser opens), and declining leaves it running.
- **Calls win.** Accepting a private call ends the listening session cleanly (a relaying phone withdraws its child with the existing route-withdrawal record, so the child re-routes). A notice offers "Listen again" after the call. A transient audio-focus loss (another app, a cellular call) mutes playout and keeps the link; a permanent loss pauses playout and keeps the link for 60 s, then leaves.
- **Disclosure** (before the first Go live and in the listening screen's info): "Anyone nearby with MeshLive can listen, including phones that pass it on. Titles from people you have not verified are unconfirmed." The broadcaster's first-use text adds: "Listeners can tell it is your phone: until streams use a throwaway identity, everyone who joins learns your phone's long-term identity key, and can link broadcasts from different days to it." Listening never starts by itself; every join is a tap.

**Audio design (Opus, 48 kHz mono).** The header already fixes 48 kHz mono Opus, every Android 10+ phone has a MediaCodec Opus decoder, libopus is already in the APK for calls, and iOS has libopus. Codec2, LPCNet or Lyra reach lower rates but have no platform decoder, no iOS path and no measured behaviour here; they are rejected for v1 because the saving (a few kbps) is smaller than the link overhead (below).

**Per-link load (planning figures; the measured values are the gate).** A mesh stream record costs hop header and tag 33 + mesh envelope 50 + endpoint header and tag 34 + inner packet header 13 = 130 bytes, plus the batch header (5 + 6 per frame). The existing 20% planning margin applies.

| **Audio Voice 24 kbps, 200 ms records** | 795 B, 5/s | **31.8 kbps** | **38.2** | **76** |

**With the per-record signature (review 1) every audio record is 68 bytes larger:** Low data 663 B (26.5 kbps wire, 31.8 planned, 63.6 relay), **Voice 863 B (34.5 kbps wire, 41.4 planned, 82.8 relay)**, Music 1463 B (58.5 wire, 70.2 planned, 140.4 relay); `MeshBandwidthEstimator.audioLive*` computes these. The other rows are the unsigned figures; the 65 kbps weak pair then carries one Voice link at 53% (plan 64%) and a Voice relay (83) still does not fit. The video rows are the app's `MeshBandwidthEstimator` figures, which count only the 13-byte packet header per record; adding the same 117 bytes of hop, mesh and endpoint framing per record would put the video rows about 37 kbps higher (about 273 kbps on the wire for the 240p default). The audio rows include all framing, so the comparison understates the audio advantage, and the audio estimator is a new function, not a reuse of `perLinkKbps` (which hard-codes 32 kbps of audio).

Against the measured context: a healthy link carries 300-1000 kbps, so Voice uses 4-13% of one link; the learned relay ceilings (430-770 kbps) are 6-10 times above what a Voice relay needs, while a 240p relay needs 567 kbps and a 360p relay 807 kbps (the measured collapse with weak relays). The 65 kbps weak pair (ASUS to A15s GATT at 45 ms carried 44-88 kbps, CoC 56-65) carries one Voice link at 49% (plan 59%) and one Low data link at 37% (plan 44%). A Voice relay on that pair would not fit (76 > 65) and a Low data relay is marginal (57 of 65). What the code does is different (review AL-5): `relayFits` compares twice the per-link figure with a transport-level learned budget, not the 65 kbps per-peer rate, and the relay cold start always grants one child. So the app will admit a relay on a weak pair; the protection is the relay child health policy plus the new shed-rate rule below. Whether a relay on the weak pair works is a test outcome (T-AL2, T-AL3), not a prediction.

**Fan-out and hops.**

- **Source.** Children = min(floor(budget / planned per-link), `audio_live_max_children`). Bandwidth alone would admit 11-18 children (428-700 kbps budgets), but the measured limits on this radio are airtime shared between connections (the M31 served one of two 300 kbps viewers and starved the other) and controller scheduling, not payload rate. Default cap **4** direct listeners: a conservative start that the fan-out ladder test (T-AL4) replaces with a measured number per transport. The existing starvation rule (a child below 50% of its offer for 8 of 10 s while a sibling is healthy is released) applies unchanged.
- **Relay.** Viewer-forwarders keep exactly one child and one parent (unchanged `ReceiverNodeForwardingCore`). Admission is the existing `relayFits(budget, perLink)` with the audio per-link figure; relay duty pauses below 20% battery as today. A two-child audio relay is phase 2 behind its own switch, because its limit is the dual-role radio schedule, not bandwidth.
- **Reach.** Cap 4 direct x (1 direct + up to 3 relayed) = at most 16 listeners at 3 relays deep on one-child relays; realistically 8-12, because relays are volunteers. Two-child relays (phase 2) give 4 x (1 + 2 + 4 + 8) = 60 at the same depth (28 at two relays). The mesh TTL (`DEFAULT_MESH_TTL` = 8) allows 7 relays and the offer route cost caps at 15; the UI shows "via N phones". The v1 gate is 3 relays (4 hops); more is measured, not promised. This is a room, a venue or a block, not a stadium: Bluetooth connections are not a broadcast medium.
- **No keyframes.** An audio-only stream has none. A joining child starts at the next record after the cached codec configuration (the relay's existing bootstrap list), so a join costs about one record of delay against 1.5-1.7 s for a video join keyframe. This is a code change, not a property of the current code (AL-12): the forwarding core calls `waitForKeyFrame()` at every child join and the forwarder asks upstream for a keyframe, so an audio-only stream skips both; the source ignores a type-5 request instead of passing it to a video encoder; codec checks and `ReceiverNodeHealthPolicy(header.fps)` get audio-only equivalents.

**Latency and jitter.** Radio is one-way: an announcement heard 1-3 s late is fine; a stall, or an hour of accumulated lag, is not.

- **Listener playout** (new profile `RADIO`, on the existing `MediaPlayoutClock`): start 600 ms, adaptive up to 3 s, shrink after 60 s without late media, catch-up with the existing pitch-preserving speed change (up to 1.06x, so a 1 s backlog clears in about 17 s). Bursts of 0.5-2 s are routine on GATT at 15 ms intervals and during relay joins; there is no picture to keep in sync, so the start delay is higher than the streams' 250 ms.
- **Bounded age (on inside this feature; per-hop only, review 15).** The 3 s and 1.5 s limits are local queue residence at one sending hop. They do not bound how old a record is when a listener plays it: delay accumulates over hops and transport buffers while each queue stays under its limit. End-to-end age is measured (T-AL3), not guaranteed. Each sending hop (source child queue, relay child queue) drops its oldest whole records once the oldest queued record is older than 3 s, down to 1.5 s, and counts them; the listener hears a short concealed gap (native decoder concealment, then the existing sender-gap silence). Reason: a stale emergency announcement is worse than a gap, and this is the only media loss in a reliable chain. It is the audio counterpart of the Experimental bounded media age (video stays no-loss by default). `test_audio_live_age_bound=false` restores unbounded queues (control arm). Shed candidates are decided by two separate exact-match predicates, one for a source's plaintext inner flags (`AUDIO` or `AUDIO | AUDIO_PAIR`) and one for a relay's sealed mesh-envelope flags (8 or 72, on an audio-only stream), because inner bit 4 (debug integrity) and mesh bit 4 (control) share a position (review 18); the header, the Opus configuration and lifecycle records are never shed (AL-11). Two safety rules apply in both arms, because the existing ones do not fit audio (AL-1, AL-2): (1) the relay child health policy reads a backlog that audio-only shedding keeps between 1.5 and 3 s, so it would never withdraw a hopeless child; for audio-only streams the child is withdrawn (rerouted) when more than 20% of its records were shed over 10 s, or when its oldest queued record is older than 30 s in the arm without shedding; (2) the sender's memory limit is bytes (8 MiB, about 35 minutes of audio), so audio-only senders also withdraw a viewer whose oldest queued record is older than 30 s. These rules get JVM tests before the sender code.
- **Estimates, to be measured:** one hop about 0.9-1.3 s (20 ms capture and encode, about 100 ms mean batch wait, queue and link 50-150 ms, 600 ms playout); each relay adds 150-300 ms (it forwards records as they arrive, with no re-batching); three relays about 1.6-2.4 s.

**Admission and capacity numbers.** Source children as above; planned wire load 38 kbps per Voice listener; a relay offers itself only if receive + forward fits; one parent and one child per relay; per-listener starvation, memory withdrawal and capacity-generation advertising unchanged. Reconnect after a parent loss uses the existing recovery path, with no keyframe wait.

**Battery.** Listener: no camera, no video decoder, no GPU, screen off. The audio track uses `PERFORMANCE_MODE_POWER_SAVING` (deep buffer) because playout is already buffered by 600 ms or more. The remaining cost is the radio link and the CPU wake for each decode. Experimental arm `test_audio_live_link_priority=balanced` asks for a 30-50 ms connection interval instead of HIGH (15 ms) on audio-only links: Voice needs about 38 kbps, a 45 ms link carried 44-88 kbps in saved logs, and the relay dual-role snoops showed that the equal-interval stage (15 ms on both sides) is where M31 children died while the 45 ms and 7.5 ms stages never did. The default stays HIGH until the A/B decides, because a 45 ms child sits at the edge of the weak-pair rate. Broadcaster: microphone and Opus encode only, no camera or video encoder, so heat and drain are far below video. No battery figure is claimed: the matched unplugged endurance adapter is missing, so energy stays UNMEASURED and only relative drain (audio-only against video watching, leaf against relay) is reported.

**Wire changes (all new, all fail closed; PROTOCOL has the normative text).**

1. **Offer marker.** In a stream offer (flag `STREAM_AVAILABLE`), `capabilitySummary` bit 6 (`0x40`) means audio only, and then the codec bits 0-1 must be zero; width, height and FPS are 0; the bitrate field is the nominal audio kbps. An older phone reads summary `0x40`, finds no decoder overlap and lists the row dimmed with its existing "not supported" label (it still counts in "N live", and a tap only shows its "cannot decode that stream's codec" notice): it cannot join, so it never opens the stream as a video. The marker is exactly `0x40` (not "bit 6 plus zeros"), which also keeps the H.264 bit 2 undefined. A reader must fail closed when the marker and the header kind disagree (`0x40` with a `BTV3` header, or a `BTL1` header without the marker), and penalises only the attempted route (address, prefix, token) for 2 minutes, plus the authenticated node ID for 10 minutes when the header came over an authenticated link; never the logical stream (review 17); a relay derives its own marker from the authenticated header, never from its parent's advertisement (AL-4). Relays and forwarders advertise the same marker for the same stream. The advertised bitrate is display only (a version 5 offer rounds it to 25 kbps units and the compact field floors it, so 16 and 24 kbps are not distinguishable there); relay admission and the trickle test use the preset in the authenticated `BTL1` header, and a zero advertised rate must not skip admission for an audio-only stream. Compact (iPhone) stream offers (the compact encoder today rejects summary bits other than codecs, so its rule and vectors change with this): codec mask 0 with resolution index 3 and FPS index 3 decodes to the same marker (specification only; an iPhone cannot broadcast yet).
2. **Stream header `BTL1`** (BME1 content type 1) replaces the `BTV3` header for these streams: eleven fixed bytes (magic, audio codec 1 = Opus, sample rate 48000, channels 1, frame ms 20, preset 1 Low data / 2 Voice / 3 Music, a reserved zero byte), then the signer block (a per-stream P-256 key, the source's identity key, and the identity key's certificate over stream ID, fixed bytes and stream key; review 1, below). No fake video fields. A phone that does not know `BTL1` fails the header read ("Unsupported stream header") and closes the link: a clean error, never a black video surface. Rejected alternative: a `BTV3` header with zero video fields would make an older parser throw a misleading "Invalid video format" and bury the stream kind in a sentinel value.
3. **Media.** Only signed audio records follow: `seq || body || 64-byte signature` around the Opus configuration, one access unit, a pair or a `BTA2` batch. A video packet, a rotation packet, an unsigned or badly signed record or a second header on an audio-only stream fails the stream closed, as does anything but exactly one 20 ms mono Opus frame per unit (up to 1275 bytes; RFC 6716 allows far more, review 14). Ingress budgets are enforced before allocation (review 13).
4. **No new endpoint hello bit, content type, or change to BMS1, BME1, BFG1 or the lifecycle records.** The offer is the negotiation: only a phone that understands the marker can select the stream.

What a 0.2.0-beta phone does: a viewer lists the row as unsupported and cannot join; a relay-capable viewer never carries the stream (it cannot join it); a source ignores audio-only offers; a forced connection (for example from a stale cache) fails at the header with a clean stop. No crash, no black screen. Test T-AL6 proves it with the real 0.2.0-beta APK.

**Security.** The same layers as video, unchanged: the signed BMH2 link handshake (BMH3 where on), ordered AES-256-GCM BMS1, a separate BME1 endpoint session with the source, and for a forwarded stream the `BFG1` grant that binds source ID and stream ID to the current media key, so every listener authenticates the original source and its immediate parent, and a relay copies sealed records without re-encrypting. The `BTL1` header is inside BME1, so it is authenticated and ordered. Risks specific to radio, and what the design does:

- *Anyone nearby can listen.* As with video, the stream is encrypted between phones but admits any phone that completes the handshake, and a relay hears it. The UI says so before the first broadcast and on the listening screen. An optional "verified contacts only" audience is Q-AL1.
- *Impersonating a trusted announcer.* A stranger can run a stream titled "Fire: use north exit". Titles from unverified people are shown as unconfirmed (advert title) or with the signed handle, a verified contact gets the existing verified mark, nothing auto-plays, and a blocked person's streams are hidden. There is no "emergency" priority flag, because anyone could set it (rejected).
- *Spam and loudness.* Every join is a tap; the app never raises volume; Block works from the listening screen.
- *Resource abuse by an authenticated source (reviews 12-14).* Age-bounded queues, a single header, and receiver-side checks done before allocation or decoding: link records over 4 KiB before the header and 28 KiB after are refused unread; records are metered (60 per second, burst 600), bytes (16 kB per second, burst 512 KiB) and lifecycle records (4 per second); every unit must be exactly one 20 ms mono Opus frame by its TOC byte; timestamps are checked as a gap rule (a step must be explained by dropped sequence numbers), a resume rule (a lifecycle record restarts the baseline) and a burst allowance (the media position may lead arrival by at most 32 s). The earlier flat "1 s step, 2x per second" rule would have rejected valid shedding and resumes and is replaced.
- *Fingerprinting.* A version 5 audio-only offer has a distinguishable layout (marker plus zero geometry); the Design E count of distinct layouts includes it. Mute keeps the microphone and its indicator on; the panel says so.
- *Metadata.* Record sizes and cadence reveal a voice stream, as they reveal video today. A listener's presence is visible to its parent, as for video.
- *Hostile relay (review 1).* A viewer-forwarder holds the media key, so BME1 alone cannot stop it forging audio that appears to come from the source; the earlier text that said it could not was wrong. The source therefore signs every record with a per-stream key certified by its identity key, and every listener and relay verifies. A forwarder can still drop, delay, withhold or replay-fail audio, and can present itself as the source (the existing BFG1 limit); it cannot alter the source's audio. Cost 68 bytes per record, about 2.7 kbps on a 200 ms record. Cheaper schemes were considered: a MAC under a source-held key needs a key the listeners also hold, so every relay could forge too; TESLA-style delayed disclosure needs synchronised clocks and delays playout; signing every fifth record over a hash chain saves about 2 kbps but adds up to a second of playout delay and a join problem. A signature per record is the simplest sound scheme at 2.7 kbps (8% of a Voice link).
- *Identity exposure (review 2).* A stream source answers BMH2 until streams move to BMH3 with a per-stream pseudonym (Design F, specified, not built), so each listener, relay included, learns the broadcaster's real key and can link broadcasts across rotating discovery IDs. This feature does not add to that exposure but does not remove it; the disclosure above says so, and the signer block uses the key that authenticates the stream, so a per-stream pseudonym drops in later without a wire change.

**Alternatives considered.**

| Alternative | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Keep "video with the camera off" | Rejected as the headline: the source still negotiates video, advertises a video offer, needs a video decoder on every viewer and 5-10 times the link budget, and an older viewer would show a black surface |
| Use the voice-call stack (BMC2, intercom) for broadcast | Rejected: calls are one peer with conversational budgets (400 ms) and the communication audio mode; the stream stack already has fan-out, relays, forwarded-source authentication and discovery |
| `BTV3` header with zero video | Rejected (wire change 2) |
| Own flag on the discovery announcement | Not possible: all eight flag bits are used; the summary byte is free for stream offers |
| A new announcement version, invisible to older phones | Rejected: versions 2 and 5 (rotating) would each need a variant, plus compact, only to hide a disabled row |
| Periodic advertising or LE Audio broadcast for unlimited listeners | Out of scope for v1: the app cannot create arbitrary BIS payloads (see "New Bluetooth and API facilities"); periodic advertising has no acknowledgement, no per-listener admission or revocation and uneven stack support. Worth a research spike after v1 as a lossy, group-keyed, in-range-only mode |
| Mixing several sources or talkers | Out of scope; group calls are Design B |
| Upgrading an audio stream to video mid-stream | Not supported: the header is fixed for the life of a stream; start a new stream |
| Recording on the listener | Not in v1 (privacy and storage); an authenticated listener can always record with another device |

**Promotion (2026-10-17, design decision).** The user asked for audio-only live on the Go live screen like voice calls, and approved turning it on by default after short phone checks instead of the full T-AL list. Checks on the 0.3.0 candidate (Voice preset, GATT, 45-90 s each): S24 to M31 direct, 2231 packets, 0 drops; ASUS to S24 to M31 relay, authenticated original=ASUS parent=S24, every record verified (0 failures), 0 drops, playback continued for over a minute with the listener's screen off; ASUS to A15s weak pair (the link that freezes 360p video), 2711 packets, 0 drops; a feature-off build next to an audio-only source neither crashed nor joined. A relay takes one listener in this version (a second listener asking for that relay waits; two is phase 2). Still open (UNMEASURED, not passed): the 10-minute pairs and other phones (T-AL1), the weak pair as relay (T-AL2), 3-relay chains and join/leave (T-AL3), the fan-out ladder (T-AL4), 30-minute background and Doze (T-AL5), the real 0.2.0-beta APK (T-AL6), interruptions (T-AL7), the age-bound A/B (T-AL8), earbuds (T-AL9) and acoustic quality. 



**Promotion criteria.** Default stays off until T-AL1 to T-AL3, T-AL5, T-AL6, T-AL7 and T-AL10 pass on the whole reference set, T-AL11 shows no regression, T-AL4 has set the fan-out cap, and the user has answered Q-AL1 to Q-AL3 (answered 2026-10-12, below). Then the one constant flips. The Experimental arms (balanced priority, adaptive rate, two-child relays, FEC, DTX) stay switchable and off; each is promoted only by its own A/B.

**Change impact (for the inventory at implementation).** The change touches the receiver, forwarder, broadcaster and sender, stream discovery and offers, the stream header decoder and the playout and audio decoder. Per AGENTS, other topologies using those stages (all stream cases and relay chains) are NEEDS RETEST on the first build that contains them, with the feature off. This design changes no existing requirement.
